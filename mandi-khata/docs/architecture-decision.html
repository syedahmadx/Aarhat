Architecture Decision · Mandi Khata

Where the data lives

Choosing the database, sync model, auth and licensing for a Windows ledger app sold shop-by-shop into Pakistani fish mandis — and the kill switch that gets you paid.

Method 12-agent evaluation · 4 opposed proposals · 3 judge panels · adversarial critique Scope Windows desktop + mobile web later

The answer

Use both, and give each one job. SQLite is the real database, living as a single file on the shop's own PC. Supabase is not your database — it is your backup target, your licence server, and the backend for your own admin dashboard.

Do not make Supabase the source of truth. Do not run your own SQL Server or Postgres VPS. And do not build a sync engine — your answers mean you don't need one.

01 What your three answers changed

You cut the hardest part of this problem out of v1 without realising it.

The evaluation ran against the hard version of your brief: phone and laptop both editing, both offline, needing to reconcile. That is a genuinely difficult distributed-systems problem, and it is where this class of project usually dies. Your answers collapse it.

DeletedBidirectional sync, and everything under it

Deferring the phone means one device writes. One writer cannot conflict with itself. That removes hybrid logical clocks, conflict resolution, op-log replay engines, and the four separate silent-data-loss bugs the critique found in that machinery. It also removes PowerSync, ElectricSQL and Turso embedded replicas from the decision entirely — you were never going to need them.

SimplifiedCloud is a mirror, not a participant

Because shopkeepers aren't holding you to a literal "it's on my machine" promise, the cloud copy can be plain readable rows rather than client-side-encrypted blobs. That matters enormously for support: when a shop phones you at 7am saying a balance is wrong, you can actually look.

KeptOffline writes are still non-negotiable

This is the one thing your answers do not remove. Pakistan had multi-day national mobile suspensions as recently as the 2024 election, and PTCL regional faults run for weeks. If the app can't record a sale with the line down, you get a furious call during the only four hours of the day that matter, and the shop reaches for the paper khata and never puts it down.

This is the whole reason SQLite stays the source of truth even though you're allowed to use the cloud. It's about the internet, not about customer preference.

Why this isn't a fudge

The two winning proposals scored 6.33 and 6.00 across three judges — a statistical tie, which means neither was right as written. Local-first won on the failure this market punishes hardest: a paid-up shop locked out at 6am. Cloud-first won on the failure that actually kills solo vendors: being unable to see a customer's data during a support call. The split above takes both, because the division of labour is clean and one-directional — the cloud never has authority over anything a shopkeeper can see.

02 Why not the two things you named

You asked "Supabase or SQL?" — both deserve a direct answer.

Why not Supabase alone

Every cloud-as-truth design ends up with an offline cliff: some number of days after which a client that hasn't checked in must go read-only, because a cache diverging for months is indistinguishable from a stolen laptop. That means a timer that can lock a customer who paid and did nothing wrong. There is no number you can pick for that timer that is safe in a market with multi-week outages.

It also makes you a single point of failure for every customer at once. A declined foreign card — routine for Pakistani issuers on recurring USD subscriptions — pauses your project, and every shop stops trading simultaneously. In the recommended split, that same event is invisible to every shop.

Why not your own SQL server

If you mean a cloud Postgres/MySQL/MSSQL box: you inherit everything above plus a permanent unpaid sysadmin job — OS patching, TLS renewal, major-version upgrades, monitoring, and the one that actually bites, proving your backups restore. You are a product developer. Every hour on that box is an hour not spent on the roznamcha screen.

If you mean a LAN SQL server in each shop — the FoxPro/Access model your competitors use — that's a second machine to install and a second thing to fail, with no phone story at all.

03 The architecture

Three boxes. In v1, data flows one way only.

Shop PC

Source of truth · Windows 10+

  • Tauri shell around your existing React app
  • shop.sqlite3 — the real database
  • WAL mode, synchronous=FULL
  • Nightly backup to USB + cloud
  • Works fully with the cable unplugged
Supabase

Mirror · licence · dashboard backend

  • Encrypted backup blobs per shop
  • shops, licenses, devices, payments
  • Readable mirror for support calls
  • One project, all shops, RLS-isolated
Vendor dashboard

Yours only · web

  • Onboard a shop, issue activation key
  • Record a JazzCash/bank payment
  • Reissue licence after hardware change
  • Read a customer's ledger, audited

v1 → shop pushes backups up. Nothing ever comes down except a licence file.  ·  v2 → phone reads the mirror. Still nothing comes down to the PC.

The upgrade path you're preserving

When you add the phone later, make it read-only against the cloud mirror. That gives shopkeepers the "check a balance from the mandi floor" value with still no conflict resolution anywhere in the system. Only if customers genuinely demand phone data entry do you need real sync — and by then you'll have revenue to pay for it. Many products never need that step.

04 The schema

Two rules here will save you more pain than everything else combined.

Money is integer paisa. Never a float.

Every amount is INTEGER paisa; every weight is INTEGER grams. Floating-point rounding in a commission calculation produces balances that are wrong by a rupee, which shopkeepers will notice and will never forgive. Write one rounding function, round-half-up, and use it for both gross and commission — the critique found a half-paisa remainder in your current 0.5 kg weight steps that silently breaks the gross = commission + expenses + net identity.

IDs are UUIDs from day one

Even without sync, autoincrement IDs make backup-restore and any future multi-device story miserable. UUIDv7 sorts chronologically and costs you nothing now.

-- Core domain (mirrors your existing AppContext shape)
CREATE TABLE parties (

  name         TEXT NOT NULL,
  name_ur      TEXT,
  type         TEXT NOT NULL CHECK(type IN ('beopari','khareedar')),

  opening_paisa INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,

  updated_at   INTEGER NOT NULL,
  deleted_at   INTEGER
);

Three things the review says to add before shop #1

Add nowParty merge

Two munshis will create "Akram Fish Corner" and "Akram Fish" within the first week. A balance split across two party records is exactly the wrong-balance catastrophe that ends trust. The merged_into column above is cheap now and painful to retrofit.

Add nowVoids as contra entries, never deletions

If voiding a sale removes its row, every khata statement you already printed and handed to a khareedar becomes wrong from that date forward. A void must post a reversing entry on today's date and leave the original struck through in place. This is what a paper khata does and what every accounting system does.

Decide nowCash in hand, and who pays commission

Every arhti counts his drawer at day end; there is no opening/closing cash balance anywhere in the current model, and its absence reads as "this software doesn't know what a day is." Separately, your mock data hardcodes 6.25% deducted from the beopari — confirm that's universal before freezing it, because per-maund commission and khareedar-borne freight both exist in this trade.

05 Sign-in when there's no internet

An account is local to the shop. The cloud never authenticates a shopkeeper.

Users live in the shop's own SQLite file, with passwords hashed using Argon2id. Your existing Malik/Munshi roles map straight onto this — the role gating you already built stays exactly as it is, it just reads from a real table instead of a dropdown.

There is no sign-up. You create the Malik account during your install visit. The Malik creates munshi accounts from a settings screen. This matches how the software is actually sold and removes an entire attack surface.

Password recovery with no email

The hard case, and it has a good answer. At install you print an Install Card — a physical card the shop keeps — carrying a one-time recovery code. A Malik who forgets their password enters that code offline. Munshi passwords are reset by the Malik, no code needed. If the card is lost, recovery goes through you: you verify identity by phone (you installed it, you know this shop) and issue a signed reset token through the same channel as a licence file.

06 Licensing and the kill switch

Two artefacts, because a signature can't be typed and a typeable key can't be verified offline.

The activation key

MK-4739-2851-6604-1927 — 16 digits plus a Luhn check digit, grouped in fours. Digits rather than letters because your users read these off a WhatsApp message in Urdu at 6am, and a check digit rejects a typo on screen instead of turning into a phone call. It's a lookup token only; it proves nothing offline.

The licence file

license.mk, about 500 bytes: a CBOR payload with an Ed25519 signature. This is what the app actually verifies at launch, with zero network.

{ v:1, lid, shop_id, shop_name, shop_name_ur,
  fp:[{name,hash,weight}…], min_score,
  issued_at, not_before,
  paid_through, grace_days:21, checkin_days:30,
  tier, seats, kid, iss }

Three details that are where these systems actually break: verify the signature over the raw received bytes and never a re-serialised struct; domain-separate by signing b"mandikhata/lic/v1" || payload; and use strict Ed25519 verification, not the permissive path.

Machine binding — fuzzy, not one hash

Hashing everything into a single value locks out paying customers on any hardware change. Score components separately and require a threshold:

ComponentWeightSource
SMBIOS System UUID4Firmware table — not WMI, whose repository is routinely corrupt on neglected installs
Baseboard serial3Same firmware table
Windows MachineGuid3Registry
Windows InstallDate2Registry — note this resets on a feature upgrade
System disk serial2Storage query
CPU family/model/cores1CPUID
C: volume serial1Win32

Pass requires score ≥ 10 of 16 and at least one weight-3-or-higher match. RAM is deliberately excluded, so a memory upgrade scores 16/16. A folder copied to another PC scores about 3/16 and is correctly blocked. Blacklist the junk values Pakistani whitebox PCs return — Default string, To Be Filled By O.E.M., all-zeroes, all-FFs — and discard those components rather than matching on them.

Drift healing: on every successful check-in, if the score passes but isn't perfect, silently re-upload the current components and reissue. This is what makes fingerprinting invisible instead of infuriating.

The single most important rule in the design

A missed check-in produces a banner and can never cause a lock. Only paid_through governs the lock. Distinguishing "has not paid" from "has not been online" is the entire game — and it's why there is no offline cliff anywhere in this design. A paid-up shop with the cable unplugged for six weeks notices nothing at all.

Clock tampering

Keep a monotonic high-water mark of the greatest timestamp ever observed, derived from the maximum of a stored file value, the newest row in the database, and the last server contact. If the wall clock reads earlier than that mark by more than a day, evaluate the licence against the mark instead. Deleting the file is not a reset, because the database floor survives it. Rolling the clock forward only expires them sooner.

Offline renewal

Primary path: you generate the new license.mk in your dashboard and send the 500-byte file over WhatsApp. Nearly every shop has WhatsApp on a phone even when the PC has no line. The munshi saves it and clicks لائسنس فائل کھولیں. Real signature, verified offline, no shared secret to steal.

Don't build thisThe "offline extension code" scheme

An HMAC-derived typeable extension code needs a long-lived secret sitting in plaintext on a deliberately unencrypted disk, and that secret generates valid codes for every future period. One technician extracts it once and sells "lifetime activation, PKR 2,000" to your entire market. Worse, the fingerprint doesn't help — it prevents copying, so this converts paying customers into permanent non-payers one at a time.

How much anti-tamper? Almost none.

Keep one clear licence-state function checked in three obvious places. Skip VMProtect and Themida entirely — they trip the ancient antivirus on mandi PCs, and that support catastrophe costs far more than the handful of people who would reverse this.

But be clear-eyed about the consequence, which the analysis initially glossed: one crack serves your whole market permanently, and every customer's binary contains the identical embedded public key. The defence isn't in the binary. Put the value in what a crack can't provide — cloud backup and restore, the phone view later, and your support. Consider delivering frontend updates only to devices whose licence check-in succeeded, so a cracked build freezes at whatever version it was cracked on and rots.

Ruling — and it isn't close

A locked shop must always be able to read, print and export its own ledger. It loses exactly one thing: creating new entries.

Commercially, blocking reads adds zero leverage — the shop can't run tomorrow's business either way, and the ledger's value to them is prospective. Reputationally, every arhti in a mandi drinks tea with every other arhti; do this once and by morning the story is "that company locked Haji sahib out of his own hisaab," and mandis talk to each other. And ethically it's the same answer: a man's udhaar book is his own record of who owes him money. You hold a copy; you don't own the record.

Make it a printed promise on the Install Card. It's a sales asset, and no competitor will offer it.

08 What I would not do

The tempting choices, and why to avoid each.

Biggest schedule riskDon't put Rust on the critical path

Tauri is Rust-based, and the natural instinct is to write your data layer there. The review's estimate for a React developer's first serious Rust project — unsafe FFI for hardware fingerprinting, lifetimes across a data engine, licence crypto, Tauri's IPC and permission model — added three to four weeks of pure language tax that appears in nobody's plan.

Instead: keep your logic in TypeScript in the WebView, use the SQL plugin for database access, and confine Rust to roughly 200 lines of fingerprint readers in a single file you write once and never touch. If performance ever demands more, move it then, with revenue in hand.

Data lossDon't move the database file between devices

Your original instinct. The moment someone copies Monday's file over Tuesday's, a day of udhaar is gone with no warning and no recovery. If you must move data manually, build explicit export/import that merges rather than overwrites — but with the phone deferred, you shouldn't need it at all.

Cost trapDon't run on Supabase's free tier in production

Free caps the database at 500 MB and flips it to read-only when you hit it — your uploads start failing while the app reports nothing wrong. Pro is $25/month, which is one shop's subscription. Pay it from shop #1 and set a retention policy for old backup blobs now rather than discovering the ceiling at shop #40.

Verify firstDon't assume Windows 7 is reachable

Rust dropped Windows 7 from tier-1 support, Tauri 2's floor is Windows 10 1803, and the Firefox ESR fallback that was supposed to cover old machines reaches end of life this month. There is no cheap Win7 path. Run winver at three real mandi shops before you write any code — if a meaningful share are on 7, your options are "a Windows 10 upgrade is a line item on the paid install" or "don't sell to those shops." Not a workaround.

09 Open questions

Decisions only you can make. The first two block schema choices.

Is commission always a percentage of gross, always paid by the beopari?

Your mock data assumes 6.25% deducted from the beopari's payout. Per-maund commission and khareedar-borne freight both exist in this trade. Confirm at real shops before freezing the schema — this is expensive to change once shops have data.

Does settlement happen per sale, or per gaari-load?

If arhtis settle by truckload rather than by individual sale, the allocation model above is the wrong shape and needs a lot/consignment entity above sales.

What are these shops switching from, and can you read it?

Every shop you win is leaving something — paper, Excel, or a competitor's FoxPro app. Nothing in this plan imports any of it, and "type in three years of udhaar by hand" loses sales.

What happens to your customers if you stop?

Worth answering before a shopkeeper asks, because they will. The strong version: issue every paying customer a sealed perpetual fallback licence at install, dated decades out, which they activate only if you disappear. It costs nothing and converts your biggest objection into a closing argument. A "trusted family member holds a USB" arrangement is a hope, not an answer.
